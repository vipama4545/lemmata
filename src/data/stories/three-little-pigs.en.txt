The Three Little Pigs
-
Once upon a time there were three merry little pigs, all with the same pink tails: Nif-Nif, Nuf-Nuf and Naf-Naf.
They spent the whole summer merrily.
Autumn came. It grew a little colder.
- Winter will be here soon - Naf-Naf said to his brothers one day. Come, let us build a house and spend the winter together, in a warm house.
The lazy piglets did not want to work.
- We'll manage in time! There is still time - said Nif-Nif.
- It is still early; I shall build a house for myself, on my own - said Nuf-Nuf.
- Me too - added Nif-Nif.
- As you wish. Then I shall build the house alone, - said Naf-Naf - and he set to work.
Nif-Nif and Nuf-Nuf spent whole days at play, while winter drew nearer and nearer every day, and only when it turned properly cold did the brothers begin to think about a house.
Nif-Nif put up a house of reeds in a single day.
Nuf-Nuf too built himself a house nearby, out of branches and twigs. On the roof he heaped dry leaves. When the brothers had finished their work, they decided to go and see Naf-Naf's house.
Naf-Naf had been building his house for several days, out of stone and clay. He hung a heavy oak door on it and fitted a bolt as well, so that the wolf from the neighbouring forest could not get in.
Astonished at the sight of the house, Nif-Nif and Nuf-Nuf cried out with one voice - is this a piglet's house or a fortress?
- A house ought to be a fortress! - answered Naf-Naf.
Nif-Nif and Nuf-Nuf turned back. Suddenly they saw a real, live wolf!
A shiver ran through the brothers and their tails began to tremble. The poor piglets froze with fear. The wicked wolf gave chase, the piglets came to their senses at once, and bolted each towards his own house.
Nif-Nif reached his reed hut first, barely made it inside, and shut the door in the wolf's face.
The wolf began to blow. Reed stalks flew off the roof of the house, and the walls of the house creaked.
The wolf blew a second time, and when he blew a third time what was left of the house flew off in all directions, as though a storm had broken over it. Nif-Nif slipped deftly out of the wolf's grasp, ran, and made straight for Nuf-Nuf's door.
The brothers had barely managed to shut the door when the wolf's voice was heard:
- Now I shall gobble up the pair of you!
Nif-Nif and Nuf-Nuf looked at each other in fear. But the wolf was very tired, and decided to use a trick.
- I have changed my mind! - he said loudly, on purpose, for the piglets to hear. - I shall not eat these scrawny piglets! Better that I go home!
- Did you hear? - Nif-Nif asked Nuf-Nuf. - He said he will not eat us! We are scrawny!
- Very good indeed! - said Nuf-Nuf, and stopped shaking at once.
The brothers cheered up greatly and, as though nothing had happened, struck up a song:
What harm can the wolf do us,
he cannot lay a finger on us,
the grey wolf, the cunning one,
who roams the woods hungry.
But the wolf had no thought of leaving at all. He simply stepped aside and hid. He wanted to laugh so much that he could hardly hold himself back from roaring out loud. How deftly he had fooled these two silly little piglets!
When the piglets had calmed down completely, the wolf took a sheepskin and crept carefully up to the house. At the door he wrapped himself in the skin and knocked quietly.
Nif-Nif and Nuf-Nuf were badly frightened.
- Who is there? - they asked, and their tails began to tremble again.
- It is me-e-e, a poor little lamb! - the wolf squeaked in a thin, strange voice. - Let me in and let me stay the night; I have fallen behind the flock and I am very tired!
- Shall we let him in? - kind Nif-Nif asked his brother.
- A lamb may certainly be let in! - agreed Nuf-Nuf. - A lamb is not a wolf, after all!
But when the piglets opened the door, it was no lamb standing there but the wolf, baring his teeth. The brothers slammed the door at once and pressed against it with all their strength, so that the dangerous beast could not burst in.
The wolf grew very angry that he had not managed to deceive the piglets. He flung off the sheepskin and roared:
- Just you wait! Nothing will be left of this house!
And he began to blow. The house shook slightly. The wolf blew a second time, a third, a fourth.
Leaves flew off the roof, the walls creaked, but the house was still standing.
And when the wolf blew a fifth time, the house fell apart; only the door stood for a while among the ruins.
The terrified piglets ran headlong. Their knees knocked with fear, every limb trembled, their snouts went dry. The brothers ran towards Naf-Naf's house. The wolf came after them in enormous strides. Once he even caught Nif-Nif by a hind leg, but he freed himself from the wolf's claws in time and quickened his pace. The wolf quickened his pace too. He was sure the piglets could not escape him now, but this time as well his luck failed.
The piglets darted past an apple tree so fast that they did not even touch it, but the wolf could not stop himself and ran straight into the tree; a great many apples came down from it. One hard apple struck the wolf between the eyes, and an enormous lump swelled on his forehead.
Meanwhile Nif-Nif and Nuf-Nuf, more dead than alive, came running up to Naf-Naf's house.
Their brother led them inside. The poor piglets were so frightened that they could not make a sound. Naf-Naf understood at once that the wolf was after his brothers. But in his own stone house he feared nothing. He quickly shot the bolt, sat down on a stool and sang out loudly:
The wolf can do us no harm,
he cannot lay a finger on us,
the grey wolf, the cunning one,
who roams the woods hungry.
But at exactly that moment a knock was heard at the door.
- Who is there? - Naf-Naf asked calmly.
- Do not talk so much! Open the door! - came the wolf's rough voice.
- What are you saying to me! I shall not even think of it! - Naf-Naf replied firmly.
- Ah, so that is how it is?! Just you wait! I shall gobble up all three of you this very minute!
- Go on then! - answered Naf-Naf, without so much as stirring from his stool. He knew that in a solid stone house neither he nor his brothers were in any danger. Then the wolf drew a deep breath and began to blow. But however much he blew, he could not shift a single small stone. The wolf turned quite blue. The house stood like a fortress. Then the wolf started shaking at the door, but the door would not give way either. In a rage the wolf began to claw at the walls of the house and gnaw at the stones, but he only broke his claws and his teeth doing it. Nothing was left for the hungry, wicked wolf but to go back to the forest with his tail between his legs. But suddenly he caught sight of a big, wide pipe on the roof.
- Aha! Here, down this pipe I shall get into the house! - the wolf rejoiced. He climbed carefully onto the roof and listened. All was quiet in the house. "Today I shall certainly eat these piglets", thought the wolf, and squeezed into the pipe. But as soon as he began to go down the pipe, the piglets heard the noise. And when soot came showering down onto the lid of the pot standing on the fire in the hearth, clever Naf-Naf understood at once what was afoot. He rushed to the pot, in which water was boiling, and took off the lid.
- Do come in! - said Naf-Naf, and winked at his brothers. Nif-Nif and Nuf-Nuf had already calmed down completely, and were gazing with happy smiles at their clever and brave brother.
The piglets did not have long to wait. The wolf, blackened like a chimney sweep, landed with a splash straight into the boiling water. His eyes started out of his head from the pain, and his whole skin was scalded. With a terrible howl the wolf shot back up onto the roof, rolled down from there, tumbled over three or four times, rolled past the bolted door and bolted off into the forest.
And the three brothers, the three little pigs, watched him go and rejoiced that they had so deftly taught the wicked, robber wolf a lesson.
And then they struck up their merry song:
Though you walk to the edge of the sky,
and travel over mountain and field,
a house as strong as ours
you will find nowhere, nowhere at all.
Lightning and wind do not frighten us,
nor are we afraid of wild beasts,
for our house is made of stone,
and a bolt is fitted on it.
After this the brothers lived together as friends under one roof.
And that is all we know about the three little pigs - Nif-Nif, Nuf-Nuf and Naf-Naf.
